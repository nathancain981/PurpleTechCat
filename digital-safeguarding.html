🛡️ Cyber Safety

What is cyber safety?

Cyber safety helps protect people, devices and accounts from online threats.

How can I keep my accounts secure?

Use strong passwords, multi-factor authentication and software updates.

What is multi-factor authentication (MFA)?

MFA adds an extra layer of security by requiring a second form of verification, such as a code from an app or text message.

What should I do if a website says my password was involved in a data breach?

Change the password immediately and use a different password for every account.

Is public Wi-Fi safe?

Public Wi-Fi can be useful, but avoid entering sensitive information unless the website uses a secure connection.

Who owns my data after I upload it to a website or app?

Different services have different terms and conditions. Some may store, process or share information in ways that people do not always expect. Always review privacy policies and settings.

Why do some companies know things about me that I never directly told them?

Information can be inferred from browsing habits, device data, app usage, purchases and online activity patterns.

Can information posted by other people affect my digital footprint?

Yes. Photos, videos, comments and tags shared by others can contribute to your online presence even when you did not upload them yourself.

How long might information remain online after I delete it?

Deleted content may still exist in backups, archives, screenshots, cached pages or copies shared by other people.

Can a data breach affect me years after it happened?

Yes. Criminals sometimes reuse stolen information for scams, fraud or account attacks long after the original breach occurred.

🎣 Scams & Phishing

How do I spot a phishing email?

Look for suspicious links, urgency and unusual requests.

What is a QR code scam?

Scammers can place fake QR codes on posters, emails or messages to direct people to fraudulent websites.

Can scammers fake phone numbers?

Yes. Some scammers use number spoofing to make calls appear to come from trusted organisations.

What is smishing?

Smishing is a scam delivered through text messages that tries to steal information or money.

Why do some scams look more convincing than genuine messages?

Criminals often copy branding, language and design from legitimate organisations to make fraudulent messages appear trustworthy.

Can criminals buy stolen personal information online?

Information exposed through breaches or criminal activity may sometimes be traded between offenders and used in future scams.

Why do scammers sometimes know my name, address or phone number?

Information can come from data breaches, public records, social media, oversharing or previous scams.

Why are younger and older people often targeted differently by scammers?

Criminals frequently adapt scams to match different interests, lifestyles, habits and communication methods.

👨‍👩‍👧 Family Online Safety

How can I help children stay safe online?

Talk openly about online activity, use privacy settings and encourage children to ask for help.

What should a child do if they feel unsafe online?

Children should stop interacting with the person or content, tell a trusted adult and seek help if needed.

Where can children get help online?

Children can speak to parents, carers, teachers, safeguarding staff or organisations such as Childline and CEOP.

What is online grooming?

Online grooming is when someone builds trust with a child online to manipulate, exploit or abuse them.

What should I do if I am worried about a child online?

Take concerns seriously, listen carefully and seek appropriate safeguarding support.

Can online games sometimes create safeguarding risks?

Many games include messaging, voice chat and social features that may involve communication with strangers.

Why is sharing school information online sometimes risky?

Publicly sharing school names, uniform photos, locations or routines may reveal more information than intended.

Can livestreaming accidentally reveal personal information?

Yes. Livestreams may unintentionally reveal locations, personal documents, routines or details visible in the background.

Why do some online services ask users to prove their age?

Age checks may be used to restrict access to content, features or services that are not suitable for younger users.

♿ Assistive Technology

What is assistive technology?

Assistive technology includes devices, software and accessibility features that help people access information, communicate and use technology more independently.

Who can benefit from assistive technology?

Assistive technology can support people with visual impairments, hearing loss, mobility difficulties, learning differences, neurodivergent conditions and communication needs.

What accessibility features are available on smartphones?

Many smartphones include screen readers, captions, voice control, magnification, colour filters, speech-to-text and text size adjustments.

What is a screen reader?

A screen reader reads digital content aloud and helps people navigate websites, apps and documents using speech or braille displays.

How can technology support people with dyslexia?

Helpful tools can include text-to-speech, dyslexia-friendly fonts, coloured backgrounds, spell checking and dictation software.

How can technology support people with mobility difficulties?

Voice control, switch access, eye-tracking technology and adaptive keyboards can help people use devices more easily.

How can technology support neurodivergent users?

Features such as focus modes, visual organisation tools, reminders, reading assistance and custom display settings can make technology easier to use.

Did you know many accessibility features were not originally designed for disabled users?

Some accessibility features became widely used because they proved useful to a much wider range of people and situations.

Why do captions benefit more people than just deaf users?

Captions can help in noisy places, quiet environments, language learning and situations where audio cannot be used.

What is cognitive accessibility?

Cognitive accessibility focuses on making information easier to understand, remember and navigate.

🚨 Reporting Cybercrime

How do I report cybercrime in the UK?

The correct organisation depends on the incident:

  • Local Police: Immediate danger, criminal activity, threats or emergencies.
  • National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC): Cyber security advice, phishing reporting and major cyber incidents.
  • CEOP: Online child exploitation, grooming and child protection concerns.
  • NSPCC: Child safeguarding concerns and support for children and families.
  • National Crime Agency (NCA): Serious and organised crime, including major cybercrime investigations.
  • National Cyber Crime Unit (NCCU): Specialist cybercrime investigations within the NCA.
  • Regional Organised Crime Units (ROCUs): Regional teams that investigate cybercrime and support police forces.
  • Cyber Resilience Centres: Help individuals, charities and businesses improve cyber security and resilience.

PurpleTechCat can provide guidance and signposting but does not investigate cybercrime.

What information should I keep as evidence?

Keep screenshots, emails, messages, usernames, website addresses, dates, times and transaction references. Do not delete evidence until you have finished reporting the incident.

How do I report a phishing email?

Suspicious emails can be reported to the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC). Avoid clicking links or opening attachments before reporting.

When should I contact my bank?

Contact your bank immediately if you think banking details, payment information or financial accounts may have been compromised.

How do I report online child exploitation concerns?

Concerns about online grooming, exploitation or abuse should be reported to CEOP or the police as appropriate.

Why are some cyber incidents investigated by specialist units rather than local police?

Some cyber incidents involve organised crime groups, large-scale attacks, international offenders or highly technical investigations. In these cases specialist organisations such as the National Cyber Crime Unit (NCCU), National Crime Agency (NCA) and Regional Organised Crime Units (ROCUs) may work alongside local police because they have dedicated cybercrime expertise and resources.

What is the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC)?

The NCSC is the UK's national technical authority for cyber security. It provides cyber security guidance, threat information and supports organisations responding to cyber incidents.

What is a Cyber Resilience Centre?

Cyber Resilience Centres help individuals, charities and businesses improve cyber security awareness, reduce risks and build cyber resilience.

🤖 AI & Emerging Technology

Can artificial intelligence imitate somebody's voice?

Yes. Some AI tools can generate realistic speech that sounds similar to a real person. This technology can be used for legitimate purposes, but it can also be misused in scams and impersonation attempts.

Can artificial intelligence create fake photographs and videos?

Yes. AI-generated images and videos can sometimes appear very realistic. It is important to verify information using trusted sources rather than relying on images or videos alone.

How can I tell whether content may have been generated by AI?

It is not always possible to tell. Warning signs may include unusual errors, inconsistent details, unrealistic imagery or information that cannot be verified from trusted sources.

Can AI-generated information be inaccurate even when it sounds convincing?

Yes. AI systems can sometimes provide incorrect or misleading information. Important decisions should be checked against reliable and authoritative sources.

Can criminals use AI in scams?

Yes. Criminals may use AI-generated voices, images, videos or messages to make scams appear more believable.

Why are some organisations creating guidance for safe AI use?

AI offers opportunities but also introduces risks involving privacy, misinformation, security and decision-making. Guidance helps people use AI more responsibly and safely.

Could somebody create an AI version of me using information I have shared online?

Public photos, videos and voice recordings may sometimes be used to train or create digital imitations. This is one reason why people should think carefully before sharing personal content publicly.

Can artificial intelligence influence the information people see online?

Yes. Recommendation systems often use AI to suggest content, videos, products and news stories based on previous online activity.

Why might two people receive different information about the same topic online?

Search engines, social media platforms and recommendation systems may personalise content based on previous activity, interests and interactions.

Can AI-generated content affect digital safeguarding?

Yes. AI can create new challenges involving misinformation, impersonation, online manipulation and scams. Understanding these risks can help people make safer decisions online.

What is a deepfake?

A deepfake is media that has been generated or altered using artificial intelligence to make somebody appear to say or do something they never did.

Could AI change how cybercrime is investigated in the future?

AI is already being used by security professionals to analyse threats, identify patterns and support investigations. At the same time, criminals may also attempt to misuse AI technologies.
